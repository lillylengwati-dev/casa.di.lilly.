What Happens When AI Gets Something Wrong?

Artificial intelligence can generate answers, organise information and support business operations. But it can also make mistakes. Discover why AI produces inaccurate information, what businesses should watch for and how to build more reliable automation systems.

Casa di Lilly Responsible AI & Business Automation 12 November 2026 10–12 Min Read
THE REALITY OF ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

Can you trust everything an AI system tells you?

Artificial intelligence can help businesses respond to customers, summarise information, prepare reports and organise everyday operations.

Some systems can even interact with connected applications and perform actions within a business workflow.

However, there is something every business owner should understand before introducing AI into their operations:

AI can get things wrong, sometimes in ways that are difficult to recognise immediately.

An AI assistant might provide an incorrect service price, misunderstand a customer request, summarise a document inaccurately or refer to information that does not actually exist.

What makes this particularly important is that an incorrect AI response can sometimes sound polished, detailed and convincing.

The goal of responsible AI automation is not to assume that technology will never make mistakes. It is to design systems that detect, limit and respond to mistakes before they become bigger business problems.

Understanding how AI errors happen is therefore an important part of building reliable business technology.

UNDERSTANDING AI ERRORS

What is an AI hallucination?

An AI hallucination occurs when an artificial intelligence system generates information that is incorrect, fabricated or unsupported but presents it as a plausible answer.

For example, a business owner might ask an AI assistant to identify a regulation or provide a reference for a business report.

The AI might produce a detailed explanation and mention a source that sounds credible. However, when the information is checked, the source may not exist or may not support the statement.

The response can look professional while still being factually wrong.

What can AI hallucinations look like?

01

Invented facts

AI provides a date, statistic, business detail or other factual statement that is incorrect or unsupported.

02

Fabricated references

An AI system generates a citation, research paper, quotation or website reference that cannot be verified.

03

Incorrect explanations

The system explains a process or subject using information that sounds logical but is inaccurate.

04

Misunderstood instructions

The system responds to a different question or interprets the user's request incorrectly.

05

Unsupported assumptions

The system fills gaps in missing information instead of explaining that it does not have enough information to answer confidently.

Researchers and AI developers use the term hallucination to describe this category of errors. NIST also discusses the related concept of AI confabulation in its generative AI risk management guidance.

WHY AI GETS THINGS WRONG

Why does artificial intelligence make mistakes?

AI systems do not automatically know whether every statement they generate is factually correct.

Generative AI models learn patterns from large amounts of information and use those patterns to produce responses to instructions.

They can generate useful answers, but this process does not guarantee that every detail is accurate.

There are several reasons AI systems can produce errors.

01

Incomplete information

The AI may not have enough context to understand the question or complete the task accurately. Instead of identifying the missing information, it may produce an answer based on assumptions.

02

Outdated information

Information about business services, software, prices, regulations and operating procedures can change. An AI assistant using older information may produce an answer that is no longer correct.

03

Ambiguous instructions

If a prompt or instruction is unclear, the AI may interpret it differently from what the user intended.

04

Inaccurate source information

If the documents or records supplied to an AI system contain mistakes, those mistakes may influence its output.

05

Misinterpretation of context

The AI may misunderstand the meaning of a message, confuse two different customers or overlook an important detail in a conversation.

06

Complex or unusual requests

Some tasks require specialised knowledge or information that is not available to the system. The AI may struggle when the request falls outside its intended capabilities.

An AI system can be very capable without being correct every time. Fluency and confidence should never be treated as proof of accuracy.

THE BUSINESS CONSEQUENCES

What happens when AI makes a mistake in a business?

An incorrect answer in an informal conversation may have limited consequences. However, when an AI system is connected to business operations, inaccurate information can affect customers, employees and business processes.

The potential consequences depend on what the system does, which applications it can access and whether anyone reviews its output.

1. Incorrect customer information

Imagine a customer asks an AI assistant about the price of a service.

If the assistant uses outdated pricing information, it may quote an amount that the business no longer charges.

The customer may form expectations based on incorrect information, creating additional work for the business to resolve.

2. Mismanaged enquiries

An AI system may misunderstand an enquiry and categorise it incorrectly.

A customer requesting technical assistance could be directed to a sales process instead of the appropriate support team.

3. Incorrect business documents

AI can help prepare draft quotations, reports, summaries and other documents.

However, if the supplied information is wrong or the system misunderstands the instructions, an inaccurate document could be produced.

4. Poor business decisions

Managers may use AI-generated summaries and recommendations when reviewing business information.

If the underlying figures or explanations are inaccurate, decisions based on them may also be affected.

5. Inconsistent customer experiences

If an AI assistant gives different answers to similar customer questions, the business may struggle to maintain consistent communication.

6. Errors that spread through connected systems

This is particularly important in automated workflows.

If an incorrect AI-generated value is transferred into another application, the mistake may affect more than one business record or process.

Connected systems should therefore include checks before important information is saved, shared or used to trigger further actions.

A PRACTICAL BUSINESS EXAMPLE

What happens when an AI assistant quotes the wrong price?

Consider a service business that uses an AI-powered website assistant to answer customer questions.

The assistant has access to business information, including service descriptions and pricing documents.

A customer asks for the price of a particular service.

Unfortunately, the connected information contains an old pricing document that has not been updated.

The AI retrieves the old information and provides the customer with an outdated quotation.

How could the business prevent this?

  1. Approved source: Maintain one current, authoritative pricing source.
  2. Information control: Remove or clearly archive outdated pricing documents.
  3. Retrieval checks: Ensure the assistant uses the correct and current information.
  4. Response boundaries: Instruct the assistant not to invent prices when information is unavailable.
  5. Approval: Require human confirmation for customised quotations or exceptions.
  6. Monitoring: Review customer interactions and correct recurring problems.

Notice that the solution is not necessarily to remove AI from customer service.

It is to improve the information, instructions and safeguards surrounding the AI assistant.

BEYOND HALLUCINATIONS

Not all AI mistakes are hallucinations.

Businesses should understand that an AI system can cause problems even when it generates factually correct information.

Some failures happen because of how the system interprets instructions, accesses information or interacts with other applications.

Incorrect output

The AI generates information that is factually wrong, incomplete or unsupported.

Wrong action

The system performs an action that does not match the intended business outcome.

Integration failure

A connected application fails, returns incomplete data or does not complete a requested operation.

Permission problem

An AI system has more access than it needs or an action is not properly restricted.

Context confusion

The AI mixes information from different requests, customers or stages of a conversation.

Instruction manipulation

Malicious or misleading input attempts to influence the AI to ignore its intended instructions.

This is why AI reliability must be considered at the level of the entire business system, not only the language model itself.

BUILDING RELIABLE AI SYSTEMS

How can businesses prevent AI mistakes?

No AI implementation can guarantee that every possible error will be eliminated. However, businesses can introduce controls that reduce the likelihood of errors and limit their potential impact.

1. Give AI clear instructions

Every AI assistant should have a defined purpose, responsibilities and operating boundaries.

Instead of simply instructing an assistant to answer every customer question, explain which information it can use, which topics it can handle and when it should refer a question to an employee.

2. Use approved and reliable information

A business should maintain accurate information about its services, prices, policies and procedures.

AI assistants should use appropriate sources for the tasks they perform.

For example, an assistant providing service information should use current approved service documents rather than relying on outdated files or assumptions.

3. Make uncertainty an acceptable response

An AI assistant should not be forced to invent an answer whenever information is missing.

Its instructions should allow it to acknowledge uncertainty and ask for clarification or human assistance.

This is particularly important for pricing, business policies, technical instructions and other information customers may rely upon.

4. Verify important information

AI-generated information should be checked against authoritative records before being used for important business purposes.

This is especially relevant for financial information, legal references, contractual terms and other consequential decisions.

5. Set appropriate permissions

An AI assistant should only access the information and applications required for its assigned tasks.

Where possible, use limited permissions rather than allowing an AI system unrestricted access to business applications.

6. Introduce approval stages

Important actions can require confirmation before they are completed.

For example, an AI system could prepare a quotation, but an authorised employee should approve it before it is sent.

7. Test the system before launch

Testing should include normal requests, incomplete information, unusual questions and examples where the correct response is to ask for assistance.

8. Monitor performance

Businesses should periodically review outputs, failed workflows, customer feedback and unexpected behaviour.

Instructions and information should be updated as the business changes.

Reliable AI is not just about choosing a capable model. It is about combining good information, thoughtful instructions, technical controls and human responsibility.

HUMAN OVERSIGHT

When should a human check an AI-generated response?

Human involvement is especially important when incorrect information could create financial, legal, operational or customer consequences.

The appropriate level of review depends on the task and the potential impact of an incorrect result.

Examples of tasks requiring human review

01

Financial documents

Draft invoices, quotations, payment instructions and financial summaries should be checked against authorised records before important use.

02

Customer complaints

Routine questions may be supported by AI, but sensitive complaints and unusual circumstances should be assessed by a person.

03

Legal and contractual information

AI can assist with organising documents, but important legal interpretations require appropriately qualified professional review.

04

Important business decisions

AI recommendations should be evaluated against actual business information and the wider context before decisions are made.

05

External business communications

Messages that contain commitments, sensitive details or customised promises should be approved according to business policy.

Human review should be meaningful. The reviewer must have enough information, time and authority to question or correct an AI-generated recommendation.

Simply approving every AI response without checking it does not provide effective oversight.

QUALITY CONTROL

How do you test whether an AI system is reliable?

Before an AI assistant or agent is used in everyday business operations, it should be tested against realistic tasks.

A successful test should not only demonstrate that the system can produce a good answer. It should also establish how it behaves when information is missing, instructions are unclear or something goes wrong.

Practical AI testing checklist

  1. Does the system provide accurate information from approved sources?
  2. Does it acknowledge when it does not know an answer?
  3. Does it avoid inventing prices, policies or business details?
  4. Does it understand the purpose of incoming requests?
  5. Does it handle incomplete information appropriately?
  6. Does it respect its assigned permissions?
  7. Does it ask for approval before restricted actions?
  8. Does it escalate complicated requests to a human?
  9. Does it handle failed integrations without falsely claiming success?
  10. Can the business review what happened when an error occurs?

Testing should continue after launch. A system that performed correctly during initial testing may need adjustments when new information, applications or business requirements are introduced.

DATA PROTECTION & SECURITY

Why data privacy matters when using AI

Many AI systems work with information supplied by businesses or retrieved from connected applications.

This may include customer details, email conversations, internal documents, business records and other information that requires protection.

Incorrect access controls or careless handling of information can create risks beyond inaccurate AI responses.

What should businesses consider?

  • Which information does the AI actually need?
  • Who is authorised to access that information?
  • How do connected AI service providers handle data?
  • What information is retained and for how long?
  • How are sensitive records protected?
  • Who is responsible for monitoring access and system activity?
  • What happens when information must be corrected or removed?

South African businesses should consider their responsibilities under the Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) when using AI with personal information.

The business should assess its legal, contractual and privacy obligations before connecting sensitive data to third-party AI services.

WHEN SOMETHING GOES WRONG

What should a business do when an AI error is discovered?

Even carefully designed systems need a procedure for dealing with errors.

Businesses should know how to identify a problem, prevent it from continuing and correct its effects where necessary.

A practical AI error response process

01

Identify the incorrect output or action

Establish what happened and which information, response or workflow was affected.

02

Pause affected actions where necessary

Prevent the same incorrect action from continuing through connected workflows while the issue is assessed.

03

Verify the correct information

Check the affected information against reliable and authorised business records.

04

Correct the affected records or communication

Follow the appropriate business procedure to correct inaccurate information and address any customer impact.

05

Identify the underlying cause

Determine whether the error resulted from inaccurate information, unclear instructions, a failed integration or another system issue.

06

Improve the system

Update the relevant instructions, information, permissions, testing procedures or approval controls.

Businesses should also maintain appropriate records of incidents and follow any applicable reporting or notification requirements.

COST & BUSINESS VALUE

Does making AI more reliable increase the cost?

Building reliable AI systems can require additional planning, testing, validation, monitoring and maintenance.

These activities may increase the initial implementation cost, but they are part of designing technology that businesses can use responsibly.

Factors that can influence the cost include:

  • Quality and preparation of business information.
  • Model and software requirements.
  • Data retrieval and verification processes.
  • System permissions and access controls.
  • Testing and quality assurance.
  • Human approval and monitoring processes.
  • Ongoing technical support and maintenance.

Not every workflow requires the same level of complexity or investment.

A simple FAQ assistant and a business-critical multi-application agent should not necessarily be designed with identical controls.

The appropriate level of investment depends on the task, the information involved and the potential consequences of an error.

RESPONSIBLE AI IMPLEMENTATION

What does responsible AI look like in a business?

Responsible AI is about more than choosing a model with impressive capabilities.

It means understanding what the system is designed to do, assessing potential risks and making sure the people responsible for it can monitor its behaviour and intervene when necessary.

A business using AI responsibly should consider:

Transparency

People should understand when they are interacting with an AI system where that distinction matters.

Accuracy

Important outputs should be supported by appropriate information and checks.

Accountability

Someone must be responsible for the system and its business use.

Privacy

Information must be handled according to appropriate access and protection rules.

Human oversight

Important decisions and actions should have appropriate human involvement.

Continuous improvement

Performance should be monitored and the system updated when requirements change.

This approach helps businesses think beyond what AI can do and consider how it should be used.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Questions about AI accuracy, mistakes and reliability.

What happens when AI gets something wrong?

When AI produces incorrect information, the consequences depend on how the output is used. An error could create confusion, provide inaccurate customer information or affect a connected business process. Important outputs should be checked and corrected using reliable information.

What is an AI hallucination?

An AI hallucination is when a generative AI system produces plausible but false or unsupported information. Examples include invented facts, incorrect references and inaccurate explanations.

Why does artificial intelligence hallucinate?

AI models generate responses based on learned patterns and available context. They can produce inaccurate information when facts are uncertain, information is incomplete or instructions are ambiguous. AI-generated fluency does not guarantee factual accuracy.

Can AI mistakes be completely prevented?

No system can guarantee that every possible error will be eliminated. Businesses can reduce risks through reliable data, clear instructions, verification, testing, restricted permissions and human oversight.

How can a business check whether AI information is correct?

Important information should be checked against authoritative and current sources. Businesses can also use approved documents, structured validation rules and human review before relying on AI-generated outputs.

Can AI customer support give customers incorrect answers?

Yes. An AI assistant may misunderstand a question or use outdated business information. Businesses should maintain approved knowledge sources, test realistic questions and provide a clear route to human support.

Should businesses allow AI to send emails automatically?

It depends on the type of email and the consequences of an incorrect message. Routine communications may be automated with appropriate controls, while sensitive or important messages should require human approval.

What is human oversight in artificial intelligence?

Human oversight means people remain involved in monitoring, reviewing and controlling AI systems. Depending on the task, this can include checking responses, approving actions, investigating errors and deciding when the system should not proceed.

How can AI errors affect business automation?

Incorrect information can move through connected workflows and affect customer records, notifications or documents. Systems should include validation, error handling and approval stages before important actions are completed.

What is responsible AI automation?

Responsible AI automation involves designing and operating AI systems with appropriate accuracy checks, privacy controls, permissions, transparency, accountability and human oversight.

Do small businesses need AI risk management?

Yes. The level of controls should reflect the business process and potential consequences of errors. Even a small business should know what its AI systems can access, which actions they can perform and who is responsible for reviewing their outputs.

How does POPIA relate to AI automation in South Africa?

When an AI system processes personal information, South African businesses should consider their applicable obligations under POPIA, including responsible information handling, appropriate safeguards and relevant data processing requirements.

THE BIGGER PICTURE

Trust in AI comes from how you design and manage it.

Artificial intelligence can help businesses organise information, improve communication and automate repetitive activities.

But no matter how advanced a system becomes, businesses should understand that incorrect outputs and unexpected behaviour are possible.

The answer is not necessarily to avoid AI. It is to implement it with reliable information, appropriate access controls, thorough testing and meaningful human oversight.

When the technology is designed around real business needs and clear responsibilities, it can provide useful support without requiring people to surrender control.

Don't just ask what AI can automate. Ask what happens when it makes a mistake, who is responsible and how your business will respond.
