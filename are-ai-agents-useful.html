Are AI Agents Actually Useful, or Is It Just Hype?

AI agents are moving beyond answering questions. They can interpret instructions, use connected tools and help complete multi-step business workflows. But what can they realistically do, and when should a business use one?

Casa di Lilly AI Automation & Business Technology 11 November 2026 10–12 Min Read
BEYOND THE AI BUZZWORD

Are AI agents genuinely useful for businesses?

Artificial intelligence has introduced another term into the business world: AI agents.

Businesses are hearing that AI agents can handle enquiries, organise information, manage workflows, interact with applications and complete tasks with less direct human involvement.

It sounds impressive, but the amount of attention surrounding AI agents can make it difficult to distinguish genuine business capabilities from marketing promises.

So, are AI agents actually useful, or are they simply another technology trend?

AI agents can be genuinely useful when they are given a clear objective, reliable information, appropriate tools and permission to complete defined tasks. They are not a guaranteed solution to every business problem.

Their value comes from connecting artificial intelligence to practical business activities, rather than simply generating another response in a chat window.

UNDERSTANDING AI AGENTS

What exactly is an AI agent?

An AI agent is a software system that uses artificial intelligence to work towards a defined goal. It can interpret instructions, determine appropriate next steps and use permitted tools to complete parts of a task.

Depending on its design, an agent can retrieve information, interact with connected applications, update records, prepare communications or coordinate a sequence of actions.

A useful way to understand the difference is to compare asking an AI a question with assigning it a task.

A simple example

A business owner asks a standard AI chatbot: "How can I organise my customer enquiries?"

The chatbot can provide suggestions and explain different approaches.

An AI agent designed for enquiry management could go further. With appropriate access and instructions, it could review a new enquiry, categorise the request, retrieve relevant service information, record the details in a connected CRM and prepare a notification for the responsible employee.

The agent is not just describing what should happen. It is participating in a defined workflow.

AI chatbot

Primarily responds to questions and generates information through a conversation.

AI agent

Can use tools and context to carry out permitted actions towards a defined goal.

HOW THE TECHNOLOGY WORKS

How does an AI agent actually work?

An AI agent combines several components to perform a task. The exact design depends on the technology and business workflow, but most practical implementations involve four important elements.

01

The AI model

The model interprets instructions, works with available information and helps determine appropriate next steps.

02

Instructions and objectives

The agent needs a clearly defined role, the outcome it is expected to achieve and rules explaining what it must not do.

03

Tools and integrations

Connected tools give an agent the ability to retrieve information or perform permitted actions through business applications, databases or APIs.

04

Guardrails and oversight

Rules, permission controls and human approval determine which actions the agent can perform and when it must pause or request assistance.

What happens when a task is assigned?

  1. A user or business workflow provides an objective.
  2. The agent interprets the instructions and available information.
  3. It determines which permitted step should happen next.
  4. It uses an approved tool or connected application.
  5. It reviews the available result and determines whether more steps are needed.
  6. It completes the task, reports the result or requests human assistance.

This process can involve several steps rather than a single response. However, the agent's ability to act depends on the permissions and tools provided to it.

KNOW THE DIFFERENCE

AI agents vs chatbots vs traditional automation

These terms are sometimes used interchangeably, but they describe different approaches to using technology in business.

1. Traditional workflow automation

Traditional automation follows predefined rules and sequences.

For example, when someone submits a booking form, the system can send a confirmation email, update a spreadsheet and notify an employee.

It is useful when the process is predictable and the required steps are already known.

2. AI chatbots

AI chatbots interact with people through natural-language conversations.

They can answer questions, explain services, summarise information and provide assistance based on their instructions and available knowledge.

A chatbot does not necessarily have permission or tools to change anything in a business system.

3. AI agents

AI agents can combine language understanding with the ability to use connected tools and perform permitted actions.

An agent might interpret a customer request, retrieve relevant information, update a CRM record and prepare a response for review.

Traditional automation

Follows defined rules. Best for predictable, repeatable processes.

AI chatbot

Understands and responds to natural-language questions.

AI agent

Can interpret goals, use tools and complete approved actions across a workflow.

Combined system

Uses AI for interpretation and traditional automation for reliable, rule-based execution.

The technologies can work together. In fact, combining them is often more practical than expecting an AI agent to handle every part of a business process.

01 • CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE

1. AI agents for customer service

Customer service is one area where AI agents can support businesses with high volumes of recurring enquiries.

Customers may ask about service availability, product information, order status, appointment procedures or general business policies.

An AI agent connected to approved business information may help interpret these enquiries and retrieve the relevant details.

What can a customer service agent do?

  • Understand the general purpose of a customer enquiry.
  • Retrieve approved service or product information.
  • Check information through authorised system integrations.
  • Categorise requests according to business rules.
  • Prepare or provide appropriate responses.
  • Direct complicated matters to human employees.

Example: A customer asks about an order. An authorised agent could retrieve the available order information, explain the current status and escalate a delivery problem to the relevant employee.

This can help create a more organised support experience without requiring an employee to manually handle every routine enquiry.

More sensitive complaints, unusual requests and decisions outside approved policies should be handed to a human representative.

02 • SALES & LEAD MANAGEMENT

2. AI agents for sales and lead management

Businesses receive enquiries from websites, email, advertisements and other communication channels.

Organising those enquiries and ensuring that they reach the right person can become time-consuming as the business grows.

An AI sales-support agent can help interpret customer enquiries and organise the information required by a sales team.

For example, an AI lead management agent could:

01

Review incoming enquiries

Interpret the customer's message and identify the type of service or product they are interested in.

02

Organise customer information

Extract relevant details and prepare a structured lead record for review or update an authorised CRM.

03

Identify the next step

Use defined business rules to determine whether an enquiry needs a quotation, consultation or human follow-up.

04

Prepare follow-up communication

Generate an appropriate draft response or notification for the responsible sales representative.

The agent can help organise the process, but pricing decisions, negotiations and commitments should remain subject to the business's approval rules.

03 • BUSINESS ADMINISTRATION

3. AI agents for everyday administration

Administrative work often involves several connected activities that require information to move between documents, spreadsheets, emails and business applications.

Some of these processes can benefit from AI agents that interpret information and use approved tools to complete defined steps.

Practical examples include:

Document processing

Retrieve information from supplied documents and organise it into structured records.

Information management

Categorise incoming information and direct it to the appropriate business process.

Report preparation

Gather approved information and prepare a draft summary for employee review.

Task coordination

Identify action items and help organise tasks in connected project management systems.

A business might use an agent to gather information from approved documents, prepare a report and notify a manager when it is ready for review.

Financial approvals and other high-impact administrative actions should retain appropriate human controls.

04 • BUSINESS KNOWLEDGE

4. AI agents for internal business information

Employees often need to search through multiple documents and applications to find the information they need.

This can create unnecessary delays, particularly when a business has several departments, services or internal procedures.

A knowledge-based AI agent can be connected to approved business documents and information sources to help employees retrieve relevant material.

Examples include:

  • Finding relevant company procedures.
  • Retrieving approved service information.
  • Summarising internal documents.
  • Finding answers within an approved knowledge base.
  • Preparing information for employee onboarding.
  • Directing employees to the correct internal resource.

An employee might ask where to find the latest approved onboarding procedure. The agent can search the connected knowledge sources and direct the employee to the relevant information.

The quality of the answer depends on the accuracy, relevance and permissions of the information available to the agent.

05 • E-COMMERCE & ONLINE BUSINESS

5. AI agents for online stores and e-commerce operations

E-commerce businesses manage several processes simultaneously, including customer enquiries, product information, order updates and support requests.

AI agents can support selected activities when connected to authorised store data and business systems.

Potential e-commerce applications

  • Answering questions about approved product information.
  • Helping customers find relevant product categories.
  • Retrieving authorised order information.
  • Categorising customer support requests.
  • Preparing draft responses about common issues.
  • Helping employees organise product-related enquiries.

For example, an agent could help a customer locate a suitable product category and retrieve relevant information from the store's approved product catalogue.

Returns, refunds, payments and other consequential actions should follow clearly defined policies and approval procedures.

06 • CONNECTED BUSINESS OPERATIONS

6. AI agents that connect different business systems

One of the more interesting capabilities of AI agents is their ability to work with different tools and applications as part of a single workflow.

Many businesses use separate systems for communication, customer management, scheduling, document storage and reporting.

When appropriate integrations are available, an agent can help coordinate steps across those applications.

Consider this business workflow:

  1. A customer submits a service enquiry.
  2. The agent interprets the enquiry.
  3. Relevant customer information is retrieved or recorded.
  4. The agent identifies the appropriate next step.
  5. A connected workflow updates the relevant business system.
  6. The responsible employee receives a notification.
  7. The employee reviews any action requiring approval.

This creates a connected process rather than requiring employees to manually transfer the same information between multiple systems.

The exact actions available depend on the applications, integrations, permissions and controls configured for the agent.

ADVANCED AI SYSTEMS

What are multi-agent AI systems?

Not every business problem needs multiple AI agents. However, some larger workflows may benefit from dividing responsibilities between specialised agents.

A multi-agent system uses several agents that can perform different tasks and coordinate their work through a defined workflow.

A simple business example

Imagine a company wants to organise its incoming sales enquiries.

A specialised system could divide the workflow into different responsibilities:

01

Enquiry analysis agent

Interprets the incoming message and identifies its general purpose.

02

Information retrieval agent

Finds relevant approved company information to support the enquiry.

03

CRM assistance agent

Helps prepare or update the relevant customer record within its permissions.

04

Communication agent

Prepares a draft response or routes the request to the responsible employee.

A coordinating workflow manages how information moves between these specialised components.

This can be useful for complex processes, but every additional component introduces more requirements for testing, monitoring and maintenance.

A single focused agent is often a better starting point than building a large multi-agent system immediately.

CHOOSING THE RIGHT SOLUTION

When should a business actually use an AI agent?

AI agents are most useful when a task requires more than a single fixed action.

They become worth considering when a workflow requires interpreting information, selecting between permitted next steps or interacting with different tools to achieve an objective.

Good situations to consider an AI agent

01

Different requests require different responses

For example, customer enquiries may need to be categorised before being directed to the correct person or process.

02

Information needs to be gathered from multiple sources

The workflow may need to retrieve relevant information from authorised applications and combine it into a useful summary.

03

The workflow involves multiple connected steps

A task may require interpreting a request, updating a record and preparing a notification.

04

Human employees need support with information-heavy work

AI agents may help prepare summaries, organise information and bring relevant material together for employee review.

NOT EVERY PROBLEM NEEDS AI

When is an AI agent unnecessary?

Although AI agents can be powerful, introducing one where a simpler solution would work can increase costs and complexity without delivering additional value.

Use traditional automation instead when:

  • A task always follows exactly the same sequence.
  • A simple trigger and action solve the problem.
  • The process does not require language interpretation.
  • The business needs highly predictable execution.

For example, sending an appointment confirmation after a successful booking does not necessarily require an AI agent.

Do not introduce an agent when:

  • The business process has not been clearly defined.
  • Reliable information is not available.
  • There is no clear owner responsible for monitoring it.
  • The proposed actions are too sensitive for the available safeguards.
  • The expected benefit does not justify the cost and complexity.

The smartest automation decision is sometimes not to use AI at all. Use the simplest reliable technology that solves the problem.

RISKS & LIMITATIONS

What are the risks of using AI agents in business?

AI agents can make useful contributions, but allowing software to take action introduces responsibilities that businesses must understand.

1. Incorrect information

An agent may misunderstand an instruction, retrieve outdated information or produce an inaccurate result.

Business-critical information should be verified through reliable sources and appropriate checks.

2. Actions taken without sufficient approval

An agent with access to business systems may be able to update records, send messages or perform other actions.

Businesses should define which actions are permitted independently and which require human approval.

3. Data privacy and access risks

Agents may interact with customer records, business documents and other sensitive information.

Appropriate permissions, access restrictions and data protection practices are essential.

4. Unexpected behaviour

An agent may interpret instructions differently from what its developer or business owner intended.

Testing, monitoring and clear limits help reduce the likelihood and impact of unexpected actions.

5. Integration failures

Connected applications may become unavailable, change their interfaces or return incomplete information.

Reliable systems should have error handling and clear procedures for situations where a connected service cannot complete its part of the workflow.

6. Cost and maintenance

AI usage, software subscriptions, integrations and ongoing monitoring can all contribute to operating costs.

Businesses should assess the full cost of maintaining an agent rather than focusing only on the initial setup.

RESPONSIBLE IMPLEMENTATION

Why human oversight still matters

An AI agent should not have unlimited authority simply because it can access a business application.

Businesses need to decide which activities an agent can complete independently, which require approval and which should always remain under human responsibility.

Examples of actions requiring careful control

  • Sending sensitive customer communications.
  • Approving refunds or significant payments.
  • Changing important customer records.
  • Making contractual commitments.
  • Deleting or permanently modifying business information.
  • Making decisions that significantly affect individuals.

A well-designed system can pause before an important action and request authorised human approval.

It should also have a way to stop, report a problem or transfer the task to a person when the situation falls outside its permitted responsibilities.

Useful AI agents are not the ones given unlimited freedom. They are the ones designed with clear responsibilities and appropriate boundaries.

PUTTING AI INTO PRACTICE

How should a business implement its first AI agent?

A successful AI agent project begins with a business process, not a software shopping list.

The business should first understand what needs to happen, why the current process needs improvement and what actions the agent should be allowed to perform.

A practical seven-step approach

01

Choose a real business problem

Identify a repetitive or information-heavy task that creates unnecessary delays or manual work.

02

Define the intended outcome

Explain exactly what the agent should achieve and how the business will recognise successful completion.

03

Choose the right tools

Identify the business applications, data sources and integrations that are genuinely required for the task.

04

Set instructions and boundaries

Define the agent's role, permitted actions, information access and situations requiring human approval.

05

Test realistic situations

Test ordinary requests, incomplete information, unusual questions, errors and scenarios outside the agent's intended purpose.

06

Launch with appropriate oversight

Begin with controlled permissions and approval processes. Monitor how the system behaves in real business situations.

07

Measure and improve

Review accuracy, task completion, time saved, errors, operating cost and employee feedback before expanding its responsibilities.

COST & BUSINESS VALUE

How much does an AI agent cost for a business?

There is no single price for an AI agent. The investment depends on the complexity of the workflow and the technology required.

A focused agent that assists with retrieving approved business information is different from a more advanced system that connects multiple applications and performs multi-step workflows.

Factors that influence the cost include:

  • Initial planning, development and configuration.
  • The AI model and usage requirements.
  • Number and complexity of integrations.
  • Data preparation and knowledge sources.
  • Hosting and technical infrastructure.
  • Security, testing and monitoring.
  • Ongoing support and maintenance.

How do you determine whether the investment is worthwhile?

Begin with the existing cost of the process. Consider how frequently the task happens, how much employee time it requires and what the consequences of delays or errors may be.

Then compare the potential operational improvements with the full cost of introducing and maintaining the agent.

Useful measurements might include:

Time saved

How much manual effort has the improved workflow reduced?

Task accuracy

How often does the process produce correct, usable results?

Customer experience

Has the process improved the organisation and consistency of customer communication?

Total operating cost

Is the ongoing cost justified by the practical value delivered?

The goal is not to invest in an AI agent because it is advanced. The goal is to introduce technology that improves a business process in a measurable way.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

AI agent questions answered.

Are AI agents actually useful for businesses?

Yes. AI agents can be useful when a business needs help with multi-step tasks involving information interpretation, connected tools and defined actions. Their value depends on the quality of implementation, accuracy, permissions and the business problem being solved.

What is an AI agent in simple terms?

An AI agent is a software system that can understand a task, determine appropriate next steps and use permitted tools to complete actions towards a defined goal.

What is the difference between an AI agent and a chatbot?

A chatbot primarily communicates through conversation. An AI agent may also have access to connected tools that allow it to retrieve information, update records or complete permitted actions across a workflow.

Can AI agents automate business processes?

Yes. AI agents can support business workflows that involve interpreting information, selecting between permitted actions and interacting with connected systems. Traditional automation can handle the more predictable steps.

Can AI agents work with CRM systems?

Yes, where compatible integrations and appropriate permissions are available. An agent may help retrieve customer details, organise enquiries and update authorised CRM records.

Are AI agents suitable for small businesses?

They can be suitable for small businesses that have a clearly defined workflow requiring interpretation or multiple connected steps. A simple workflow automation may be more appropriate for straightforward tasks.

What is agentic AI?

Agentic AI refers to AI systems designed to pursue goals and carry out tasks with a degree of autonomy. This may involve planning, using tools, responding to information and coordinating multiple steps within defined boundaries.

Can multiple AI agents work together?

Yes. Multi-agent systems can divide responsibilities between specialised agents and coordinate their work. They are generally more complex to design, test and maintain than a single-agent solution.

Can AI agents make mistakes?

Yes. Agents can misunderstand instructions, retrieve inaccurate information or perform an inappropriate action within their available permissions. Testing, monitoring, guardrails and human approval help manage these risks.

Do AI agents replace employees?

AI agents can support or automate selected tasks, but they do not replace the full range of human capabilities. Employees remain important for judgment, accountability, complex decisions and customer relationships.

How much does it cost to build an AI agent?

Costs depend on the model, workflow complexity, connected applications, development, hosting, testing and ongoing maintenance. Businesses should assess the total operating cost against the expected practical value.

Can AI agents be used by South African businesses?

Yes. South African organisations can explore AI agents for customer service, lead management, document workflows, internal information and business operations. Their implementation should account for appropriate security, privacy and applicable data protection obligations.

THE BIGGER PICTURE

AI agents are useful when they have a purpose.

AI agents are more than a new label for chatbots. They represent a way of connecting artificial intelligence to tools, information and defined business activities.

They can support customer service, sales assistance, administration, knowledge retrieval and connected business workflows.

But not every task needs an AI agent, and not every business is ready to introduce one.

The most important step is identifying a genuine business challenge and selecting technology that can address it reliably.

Don't introduce AI agents because everyone is talking about them. Introduce them when they can make a real business process work better.
